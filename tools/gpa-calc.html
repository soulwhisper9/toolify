Frequently asked questions

How is GPA actually calculated?
Multiply each course's grade points by its credit hours to get quality points. Add all quality points, then divide by total credit hours. A 3-credit A (4.0) and a 1-credit C (2.0) give (12 + 2) ÷ 4 = 3.5, not the simple average of 3.0.
What is the difference between GPA and CGPA?
GPA covers a single semester. CGPA is cumulative across every semester you have completed. Enter your previous CGPA and total prior credits above and this tool works out the updated figure.
Do the letter grades match my university?
This uses the common 4.0 scale where A = 4.0, A− = 3.7, B+ = 3.3 and so on. Some institutions cap A at 4.0 with no A+, and a few use 5.0 scales. Check your handbook, and use the grade points input if your scale differs.
Do failed or repeated courses count?
Usually an F counts as 0.0 and still consumes credit hours, which drags the average down. Many universities let a repeat replace the original grade. Include only what your transcript actually counts.
