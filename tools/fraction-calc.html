Frequently asked questions

How do you add fractions with different denominators?
Find a common denominator by multiplying the two denominators (or use the LCM for smaller numbers). Convert both fractions, add the numerators, then simplify. For 1/2 + 1/3 the common denominator is 6, giving 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.
Why does dividing flip the second fraction?
Dividing by a number is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal. So a/b ÷ c/d becomes a/b × d/c. This is often taught as "keep, change, flip".
What is a mixed number?
A whole number combined with a proper fraction, such as 1 2/3. Any improper fraction where the numerator exceeds the denominator can be rewritten this way.
How does simplification work?
Both numerator and denominator are divided by their greatest common divisor. For 8/12 the GCD is 4, so it reduces to 2/3.
