Frequently asked questions

Sample or population — which do I need?
Use the population formula only when your data covers every member of the group you care about, such as marks for an entire class. Use the sample formula when your data is a subset used to estimate a wider group. Coursework usually wants the sample version.
Why divide by n − 1 for a sample?
This is Bessel's correction. A sample tends to cluster closer to its own mean than the true population mean, which underestimates spread. Dividing by n − 1 instead of n compensates and gives an unbiased estimate.
Why square the deviations?
Deviations above and below the mean cancel out and would always sum to zero. Squaring makes them all positive, and also gives larger deviations more weight. The square root at the end returns the answer to the original units.
What counts as a high standard deviation?
It depends on scale, which is why the coefficient of variation is shown. That expresses standard deviation as a percentage of the mean, making it comparable across data sets with different units.
