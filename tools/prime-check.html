Frequently asked questions

What makes a number prime?
A prime has exactly two distinct divisors: 1 and itself. 7 is prime because nothing between 2 and 6 divides it evenly. 8 is not, because 2 and 4 both divide it.
Why is 1 not prime?
It has only one divisor, not two. Excluding it keeps the fundamental theorem of arithmetic clean: every integer above 1 has exactly one prime factorisation. If 1 counted, you could pad any factorisation with endless 1s.
Why only check up to the square root?
Divisors come in pairs. If n = a × b, one of them must be at most √n. So finding no divisor by the square root proves none exists at all, which makes the check far faster.
What are prime numbers used for?
Modern encryption depends on them. RSA relies on the fact that multiplying two large primes is easy but factoring the product back is extremely hard. Primes also appear in hashing and random number generation.
