Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between independent and mutually exclusive?
Independent means one event does not affect the other, so P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Mutually exclusive means they cannot both happen, so P(A and B) = 0 and P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B). Two events cannot be both, unless one has zero probability.
How does conditional probability work?
P(A given B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B). It narrows the sample space to only the outcomes where B happened, then asks how often A also occurred.
What are repeated trials used for?
The binomial formula answers questions like "what is the chance of exactly 2 sixes in 10 dice rolls". It needs a fixed number of trials, the same probability each time, and independent outcomes.
Why must probability sit between 0 and 1?
0 means impossible and 1 means certain. Anything outside that range signals an input error. Multiply by 100 to express it as a percentage.
